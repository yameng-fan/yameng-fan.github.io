Working Paper

A Market Solution under Food Security Constraints: Evidence from a Policy Experiment in China (Job Market Paper)

Presentations: 2026 Asia Meeting, East-Southeast, Hanoi, Vietnam; the 4th Summer Meeting in Urban Economics China (WHU); 2026 AsRES-GCREC Joint International Conference, Hangzhou; 2026 CES China Annual Conference, Southwestern University of Finance and Economics; Xi'an Jiaotong University PhD Workshop; HKUST-UW Workshop on Climate Risk, Sustainability and Real Estate; HKU PhD Brownbag; World Bank Land Conference 2024, Washington, DC.

Abstract

Food-security concerns often motivate land-use regulations that preserve farmland but restrict urban expansion and labor reallocation. This paper studies the Chongqing Land Coupon Reform, a market-based policy in China that allows rural construction land reclaimed as farmland to generate tradable land coupons, which developers can use to convert peri-urban farmland into urban construction land while maintaining aggregate farmland. Using comprehensive transaction-level data, I document that coupon supply comes primarily from remote rural counties, while demand is concentrated in urban fringe counties. Counties with lower initial agricultural value added per worker supply more coupons and experience higher rural-to-urban migration, and coupon-purchasing counties experience faster grain-yield growth. To quantify the aggregate effects, I develop a quantitative spatial model with heterogeneous agricultural productivity, endogenous migration, regulated land conversion, and a land coupon market. The results show how market-based land reallocation can mitigate the distortions created by food-security-driven land regulations.
