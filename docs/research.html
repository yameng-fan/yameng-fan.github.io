Structural Change and Food Security Constraints: A Market Solution

Job Market Paper

Presentations: 2026 Asia Meeting, East-Southeast, Hanoi, Vietnam; the 4th Summer Meeting in Urban Economics China, Wuhan University; 2026 AsRES-GCREC Joint International Conference, Hangzhou; 2026 CES China Annual Conference, Southwestern University of Finance and Economics; Xi'an Jiaotong University PhD Workshop; HKUST-UW Workshop on Climate Risk, Sustainability and Real Estate; HKU PhD Brownbag; World Bank Land Conference 2024, Washington, DC.

Abstract

Food-security concerns often motivate land-use regulations that preserve farmland but restrict urban expansion and labor reallocation. This paper studies the Chongqing Land Coupon Reform, which allows reclaimed rural construction land to generate tradable development rights for urban land conversion while preserving aggregate farmland. Using comprehensive transaction records, I document that coupons flow primarily from remote rural counties to urban-fringe areas. Descriptively, counties with lower initial agricultural value added per worker subsequently supply more coupons and exhibit greater rural-to-urban migration, while greater coupon supply is associated with faster grain-yield growth. I develop a quantitative spatial model with heterogeneous farming ability, endogenous migration, regulated urban land conversion, and a land coupon market. Eliminating the market reduces urban residential land by 14.0 percent and raises housing rents by 15.1 percent. It reduces aggregate rural-to-urban migration and agricultural labor productivity by 0.58 and 0.59 percent, respectively, and raises the shadow value of the urban land quota from 300 to 2,336 yuan per square meter. The results show that tradable development rights operate primarily by relaxing urban land constraints, with additional gains through migration and agricultural labor reallocation.
